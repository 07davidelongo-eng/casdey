casdey

brand guide · v3 · iron & brass · august 2026

Matte, not shiny. Earned, not flashy.

casdey is deliberately generic, so the identity can outlast a pivot without touching the domain, the email or the name. v3 is that pivot: the niche moved from dental practices to gyms and studios (see CLAUDE.md, "Niche pivot under consideration"), and the identity moved with it.

What changed from v2, and why. v2 was warm neutrals on a light ground with a calm teal primary, right for a dental patient's inbox: reassuring, clinical, quiet. A gym's register is intensity and discipline, not reassurance, so v3 flips the ground dark and swaps teal for a worked brass, built from the weight room's own material world (cast iron, worn barbell knurling, brass fittings, chalk) rather than the shiny "elite gym" gold every boutique studio's Instagram funnel already uses. The wordmark does not move. That is the one thing the brand guide promises survives every pivot.

01 · wordmark

Lowercase, always. Even mid-sentence.

"casdey" is never capitalised. Not at the start of a sentence, not in a headline, not in a signature. Treat it as a fixed spelling, not a style choice to be turned on and off. It is set in Raleway Light and it is the only place Raleway appears. Unchanged from v2, including on the now-dark ground: the wordmark was always drawn to reverse out of a dark plane, that reversed treatment is simply the default now instead of the exception.

rules

Chalk #F1ECDF on Iron or Iron Raised

Clearspace of one lowercase "c" height on every side

Never capitalise any letter

Never recolour outside Chalk, Iron or Iron Raised

Never add a shadow, outline or gradient

Never stretch, condense or re-kern the letterforms

02 · colour

A ground with a warm undertone, one worked metal.

Iron replaces Paper as the ground: near-black, but biased warm and brown rather than neutral grey, so it reads as a chosen material and not a generic dark-mode default. Brass replaces Teal as the primary, always matte, never a bright yellow-gold. Every token keeps its v2 name, only the hex values moved, see globals.css for why.

Chalk on Iron clears 15:1. Brass on Iron clears 6:1, both pass AA for body text.

Dark text (Iron) on Brass or Brass Bright also passes AA, use it for text sitting on a filled Brass surface.

Never a bright yellow-gold for Brass. Desaturate before it reads as jewellery.

Never assume a token still means what its name says. --white is no longer literally white, it is the raised surface, see globals.css.

04 · the signature

One member's history as a line.

Two visits close together, a long dashed nothing, then a booking. Same shape as v2, it already did the job, it just needed the right metal: Amber swapped for Brass, "rebooked" swapped for "rejoined." Use it in a hero, on the waitlist page, or anywhere the gap needs explaining without a paragraph.

05 · surfaces and depth

Three planes, never four.

BaseIron. The page itself. No border, no shadow.
RaisedIron Raised, 22px radius, 1px Ash border at 60%, layered black shadow. Cards, panels, the form.
FloatingSame but with the heavier shadow, and it overlaps something. Used once per screen at most.
InvertedDeep, full-bleed or 28px radius. Steps, calls to action. Two per page maximum. Text on Deep is Chalk or Sea, never the raised-surface token, that is the one mistake this palette makes easy, see the note in section 02.
Radius10px controls · 14–16px small tiles · 20–22px cards · 28px full-bleed blocks
ShadowsLayered black at low opacity, never a single flat layer, never petrol-tinted (that was v2's ground colour, not this one's).
MotionTransform and opacity only, 200–220ms, cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1). Never transition-all. Reduced motion is respected.

06 · voice

Plain, direct, a little dry.

Unchanged by the pivot. The niche moved, the way casdey talks did not.

Casingcasdey, lowercase, always, no exceptions for grammar.
PunctuationNo em dashes as a comma substitute. A sentence ends and the next one starts.
Sign-offDavide @casdey on one line, no "Best," preamble.
Guarantee"if it doesn't recover more than it costs, you don't pay"
ToneStates the problem plainly, offers the fix, gets out of the way. Never asserts facts about the reader's own business.
EvidenceNo invented statistics, ever. If a number is not real, it does not go on the page. Diagrams that show a shape rather than data say so on the diagram.

casdey

brand guide · v2 · august 2026

Quiet name, warmer system.

casdey is deliberately generic, so the identity can outlast a pivot without touching the domain, the email or the name. The mark stays quiet on purpose. Everything around it now carries more weight than it did in v1.

What changed from v1, and why. v1 was greyscale with a single slate accent. At the scale of a full page it read cold, thin and beige, and the one accent could not carry a hero, a dark band and a call to action at the same time. v2 keeps the neutral bones and the wordmark exactly as they were, then adds a petrol ground, a teal primary, two tints and one warm accent. Headlines moved off Raleway, which was always meant for the wordmark and nothing smaller.

01 · wordmark

Lowercase, always. Even mid-sentence.

"casdey" is never capitalised. Not at the start of a sentence, not in a headline, not in a signature. Treat it as a fixed spelling, not a style choice to be turned on and off. It is set in Raleway Light and it is the only place Raleway appears.

rules

Ink #0D0D0D on Paper or White

Paper #FAF9F7 reversed out of Deep

Clearspace of one lowercase "c" height on every side

Never capitalise any letter

Never recolour outside Ink, Paper or White

Never add a shadow, outline or gradient

Never stretch, condense or re-kern the letterforms

02 · colour

Warm neutrals, a petrol ground, one warm accent.

The neutrals come from the logo file itself. The petrol and teal do the work the old single accent could not. Amber appears once per page at most: it marks the moment a dormant patient books again, and nothing else. It is never a button and never a background.

03 · typography

Four faces, one job each.

Raleway carries the wordmark and nothing smaller: its warmth reads as considered at logo size and precious everywhere else. Familjen Grotesk took over headlines in v2, because a page needs a display face with more presence than Raleway Light can give it. Inter takes everything that gets read. 04 · the signature

One patient's history as a line.

Two visits close together, a long dashed nothing, then a booking. It is the whole product in one picture, and it is the only place Amber is allowed to appear. Use it in a hero, on a waitlist page, or anywhere the gap needs explaining without a paragraph.

05 · surfaces and depth

Three planes, never four.

BasePaper. The page itself. No border, no shadow.
RaisedWhite, 22px radius, 1px Ash border at 60%, layered petrol-tinted shadow. Cards, panels, the form.
FloatingSame but with the heavier shadow, and it overlaps something. Used once per screen at most.
InvertedDeep, full-bleed or 28px radius. Steps, calls to action. Two per page maximum.
Radius10px controls · 14–16px small tiles · 20–22px cards · 28px full-bleed blocks
ShadowsAlways tinted with petrol, never flat black, always two layers or more.
MotionTransform and opacity only, 200–220ms, cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1). Never transition-all. Reduced motion is respected.

06 · voice

Plain, direct, a little dry.

Casingcasdey, lowercase, always, no exceptions for grammar.
PunctuationNo em dashes as a comma substitute. A sentence ends and the next one starts.
Sign-offDavide @casdey on one line, no "Best," preamble.
Guarantee"if it doesn't recover more than it costs, you don't pay"
ToneStates the problem plainly, offers the fix, gets out of the way. Never asserts facts about the reader's own business.
EvidenceNo invented statistics, ever. If a number is not real, it does not go on the page. Diagrams that show a shape rather than data say so on the diagram.

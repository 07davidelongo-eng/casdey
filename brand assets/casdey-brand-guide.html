brand guide · v4 · chalk & struck gold · september 2026

Earned, not flashy. Gold as a result, not a finish.

casdey is deliberately generic, so the identity can outlast a pivot without touching the domain, the email or the name. v3 rebuilt it in an afternoon to catch the gym pivot. v4 is the considered version: the gym product stayed, the register changed, and the identity finally has a mark rather than a word on its own.

What changed from v3, and why. v3 was a dark iron ground with a matte brass primary. It was not wrong, it was fast: a same-day reaction to the pivot rather than a decision. v4 flips the ground back to light and commits to gold, and makes two choices worth defending. The ground is cool, not cream: warm cream under gold is the most predictable pairing available, and it would have made the gold read as ambient warmth instead of intent. Holding the neutrals slightly cool leaves gold as the only warm thing on the page. And the gold is struck, not shiny, for the same reason it was brass before: what casdey sells is recovered revenue, a number a gym wins back, so the primary is the earned mark rather than the luxury finish every boutique studio's Instagram funnel already uses. The wordmark stays lowercase. That is the one thing the brand guide promises survives every pivot.

01 · logo

Lowercase, always. Even mid-sentence.

"casdey" is never capitalised. Not at the start of a sentence, not in a headline, not in a signature. Treat it as a fixed spelling, not a style choice to be turned on and off. It is set in Outfit, the same face as every headline, which is new in v4: v2 and v3 gave the wordmark a family of its own (Raleway) used for exactly one word, and folding it into the display face costs nothing and makes the identity cohere.

v4 also adds a mark, because the wordmark alone is illegible below about 100px and a favicon had to fake one. The mark is four bars pinwheeled around an open centre, one of them gold. It is abstract on purpose. It does not spell the name and it does not illustrate what casdey does, because the first version that tried to do both (a ring with a gap, closed by a gold arc) only held together in two colours and fell back to a plain ring in one. Four bars of real weight, with an equal gap at all four corners, still read as a square at 16px, which is the size that decides whether a mark works.

rules

Ink #15150F on Chalk or White, or Chalk reversed out of Deep

Clearspace of one lowercase "c" height on every side

Exactly one bar is gold, it is Leaf #D4AF37, and it is the only colour in the mark

Never capitalise any letter

Never recolour the gold bar, never rotate the mark off gold-at-the-top, and never let the wordmark take the gold

Never add a shadow, outline or gradient

Never stretch, condense or re-kern the letterforms

02 · colour

A cool ground, so one warm thing can matter.

Chalk replaces Iron as the ground: an off-white pulled slightly cool and green, deliberately away from the warm cream that gold is usually paired with, so gold stays the only warm thing on the page. Gold arrives in two values because one cannot do both jobs: any gold light enough to look like gold is too light to be read as body text on Chalk. Struck Gold is the value that gets read, Leaf is the value that fills. Every token keeps its v2 name, only the hex values moved, see globals.css for why.

Ink on Chalk clears 17:1. Struck Gold on Chalk clears 4.6:1, and holds white at 5.0:1. Both pass AA for body text.

Leaf is a fill, never small text on Chalk: it only clears 2:1 there. Put Ink on it instead, which clears 8.7:1.

On Deep, the primary becomes Leaf (8.6:1). globals.css does that swap for you inside .on-deep, so do not hand-pick it.

Never set Leaf as body copy on a light ground, however much more like gold it looks.

Never assume a token still means what its name says. --white is no longer literally white, it is the raised surface, see globals.css.

03 · typography

Three families, four roles.

v3 carried four families, one of them (Raleway) spent on a single word. v4 folds the wordmark into the display face, which costs nothing and makes the logo stop being a stranger to the headline under it. Outfit carries every title and the wordmark. IBM Plex Sans takes everything that gets read, and it is chosen against Outfit rather than to match it: an engineered grotesque set beside round geometry is a contrast of temperament, not two neutral sans faces stacked, and it was drawn for dense interface data, which the member and campaign tables need. IBM Plex Mono, its own sibling, marks anything literal.

04 · the signature

One member's history as a line.

Two visits close together, a long dashed nothing, then a booking. The shape has survived every version because it does the job. It matters more in v4 than it did before: now that the mark is abstract, this is the only place in the identity that draws the gap at all. Use it in a hero, on the waitlist page, or anywhere the gap needs explaining without a paragraph.

05 · surfaces and depth

Three planes, never four.

BaseChalk. The page itself. No border, no shadow.
RaisedWhite, 22px radius, 1px Ash border at 60%, layered ink-tinted shadow. Cards, panels, the form. On Chalk a card lifts by being whiter than the ground, not by being greyer.
FloatingSame but with the heavier shadow, and it overlaps something. Used once per screen at most.
InvertedDeep, full-bleed or 28px radius. Steps, calls to action. Two per page maximum. Always mark the container .on-deep: globals.css redefines the neutral and gold roles inside that class, so text-ink, border-ash and the primary resolve to their dark-plane values on their own. Never hand-pick a light token instead, and never nest a light plane inside it.
Radius10px controls · 14–16px small tiles · 20–22px cards · 28px full-bleed blocks
ShadowsLayered and tinted with Ink's warm black at low opacity, never a single flat layer and never pure black, which reads as a grey box sitting on chalk rather than light falling on it.
MotionTransform and opacity only, 200–220ms, cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1). Never transition-all. Reduced motion is respected.

06 · voice

Plain, direct, a little dry.

Unchanged by the pivot. The niche moved, the way casdey talks did not.

Casingcasdey, lowercase, always, no exceptions for grammar.
PunctuationNo em dashes as a comma substitute. A sentence ends and the next one starts.
Sign-offDavide @casdey on one line, no "Best," preamble.
Guarantee"if it doesn't recover more than it costs, you don't pay"
ToneStates the problem plainly, offers the fix, gets out of the way. Never asserts facts about the reader's own business.
EvidenceNo invented statistics, ever. If a number is not real, it does not go on the page. Diagrams that show a shape rather than data say so on the diagram.

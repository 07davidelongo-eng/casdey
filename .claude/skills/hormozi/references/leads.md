## What a lead is

A lead is anyone you can contact. That's not enough. The real target is an
**engaged lead** — someone who has *shown* interest (replied, opted in,
booked, followed and reachable). "Engaged leads are the true output of
advertising." The book's job: get more of them, reliably, from several
sources. Growth = more customers × worth more; this book is the "more
customers" half, via more / better / cheaper / reliable engaged leads.

## Lead magnets — the seven steps

A lead magnet is a **complete solution to one narrow problem**, given free or
near-free, that (a) delivers a real quick win and (b) reveals the next problem
— which your core offer solves for money. ("Salty pretzels → thirst → drink.")
It should be something people would pay for. A person who pays with *time*
now is likelier to pay with *money* later. A paid budget to deliver it (even
$25) lets you out-value everyone giving away $0 fluff.

1. Pick the narrow, meaningful problem and who it's for (must reveal a
   problem your core offer solves).
2. Figure out how to solve it.
3. Figure out how to deliver it — three types (reveal-the-problem /
   sample-of-the-service / one-of-many-steps) × four delivery methods →
   up to a dozen versions; rotate them.
4. Test the **name/headline** (headline first, then image, then subheadline).
   "How to…" beat "…" ; fewer words beat more. Run polls; if people ask when
   they can get it, you have a winner.
5. Make it easy to consume (every format, every device).
6. Make it genuinely good — "give away the secrets, sell the implementation."
   99% won't buy but they judge your reputation on the free thing.
7. Make it easy to say "I want more" — a clear CTA (**what to do** + **a
   reason to do it now**: scarcity, urgency, or literally any stated reason —
   "because…").

## The Core Four

Every way one person can get a lead is one cell of a 2×2: **who** (people who
know you = warm / strangers = cold) × **how many at once** (private 1-to-1 /
public 1-to-many).

|          | 1-to-1 (private)   | 1-to-many (public)   |
| -------- | ------------------ | -------------------- |
| **Warm** | Warm outreach      | Post free content    |
| **Cold** | Cold outreach      | Paid ads             |

You only need **one** working to a predictable leads-per-day before adding the
next. Adding a channel too early splits attention across two things that don't
work. What to do when: after warm outreach seeds a customer base — if you have
more time than money, post content; more money than time, cold outreach or
paid ads. They compound (a business doing content + ads gets more from each
than a business doing either alone).

Common shape across all four: personalise/target → **big fast value in the
hook** → one clear CTA → **follow up** (most results come from follow-ups;
multi-touch, multi-channel, then a final message) → measure the funnel
(contacted → replied → positive → booked → bought), not a vanity metric.

## Warm outreach (ACA)

1-to-1 to people who know you. Cheapest, most reliable, needs no audience or
budget — the default *starting* channel and the way to get your "first five
clients". Everyone has a list (phone, every old email account, every social
platform → often 1,000+ contacts). Reach out to **100 per day**, up to 3
times each (once/day for 3 days or until they reply).

The **ACA** conversation loop, once they respond:
- **Acknowledge** — restate what they said in your words (active listening).
- **Compliment** — tie it to a positive character trait.
- **Ask** — a question that steers toward your topic.

Then make the ask as a *referral* question, not a buy question: "do you know
anybody who [struggle] wants [dream outcome] in [time]? I'm taking 5 free
case studies for testimonials. I help them [dream outcome] without [effort],
I guarantee [outcome] or I keep working. [testimonial like them]. Anyone come
to mind?" You're not asking them to buy — you're asking who they know. Most
who say "yes, me" are interested; it never feels pushy. On email you compress
it: 2–3 personalised sentences, then straight to the offer/lead magnet.
Rewriting scripts below a 3rd-grade reading level lifted email replies ~50%.

## Post free content (give:ask)

1-to-many to a warm (and warming) audience. Slow to start, compounds, builds
an owned audience and trust at scale. "Hook, retain, reward." Give value
repeatedly, then occasionally ask. Lessons that matter for a small business:
- **"How I" not "how to"** — talk about your experience, not what others
  should do; it's unquestionable.
- **We need reminding more than teaching** — repeat yourself; you'll bore of
  it long before the audience has all seen it.
- **Puddles → ponds → lakes → oceans** — narrow the topic to exactly what you
  do and where; become king of the puddle first.
- **Content feeds sales** — keep a labelled "greatest hits" list reps can send
  before/after calls to resolve specific objections.
- Measure audience **size** and **growth rate** monthly; control inputs (a
  posting cadence you can hold), not outputs.

## Cold outreach (list, message, volume)

1-to-1 to strangers. Works with no audience and no ad budget — just effort, a
list, and a message. Sits on top of warm-outreach skill. Strangers add three
problems: no way to contact them, they ignore you, they're not interested.
Solve in that order.

1. **Build a list** — most→least accessible: (a) scraping software, test a few
   hundred from each; (b) list brokers, test sample lists; (c) elbow grease —
   join groups/communities, pull contacts manually. Self-assembled lists are
   the *freshest* (fewer competitors have hit them). Interest groups are "the
   warmest cold audience."
2. **Message** — two things carry it: **personalisation** (1–3 things a friend
   might know, complimented, ideally showing how it helped you — make the cold
   touch feel warm) and **big fast value** (not "tickle interest" — blow their
   mind in <30 s; give away something people *actually pay for*). Swapping a
   "game planning session" (a disguised sales call) for genuinely free service
   3x'd take rates. Keep scripts short (phone/chat 1–2 pages, email ≤ half a
   page); ship 100 conversations / 10,000 emails before tweaking.
3. **Volume** — automate delivery (pre-recorded / templated), automate
   distribution (blast), and **follow up more times in more ways**
   (email → then a call/DM "following up on my email" — a reply or a reason to
   reach out again, you win either way). Expect 2–3 conversations before a
   higher-ticket close. Personalisation × volume is the deadly combo; tech is
   closing the gap between them.

Cold outreach took Hormozi ~a year to scale (not the 12 weeks he guessed) and
needed someone who'd done it before; early months looked like failure.

## Paid ads (What-Who-When, phases, LTGP:CAC, CFA)

1-to-many to strangers. Fastest to scale, costs money, needs a working offer
first. Efficiency matters more than creativity — "all advertising works, only
how well differs." Do this channel **last** (skills and money from the other
three transfer in).

Every ad has three parts: **call-outs** (so the right people notice it),
**value elements** (a reason to act now), **CTA** (a way to act). The value
copy uses **What-Who-When**:
- **What** — hit all four value-equation drivers *and* their opposites
  (dream/nightmare, likelihood/risk, speed/slowness, ease/effort).
- **Who** — status: show how the people around them (spouse, kids, peers,
  rivals, boss) treat them differently after they buy.
- **When** — run each through their past / present / future timeline.
Longer ads just cover more angles; keep call-outs and CTA constant.

**Three phases of spend**: Track money (set up tracking before spending a
dollar) → Lose money (you'll lose more *times* than you win; cap a test at
2× the 30-day cash from a customer, kill a no-lead ad before 1×) → Print money
(once returns > spend, stop asking "how much to spend?" and ask "how many
customers can I handle?", then reverse the budget from that, +20%).

**LTGP : CAC** — lifetime *gross profit* (all revenue minus all cost to serve,
over the whole relationship) over fully-loaded cost to acquire. Aim **≥ 3:1**
(pattern Hormozi observed, not a law: businesses that can't scale are almost
always below 3:1). Two levers: lower CAC (better ads) or raise LTGP (better
business model). Use *industry-average CAC* as the tell: if your CAC is below
3× the industry average, your problem is the business model (LTGP); above 3×,
it's your ads. Costs bottom out near zero; LTGP has no ceiling — eventually
you just have to make more per customer. Don't confuse a **sales** problem
(right leads, not buying) with an **advertising** problem.

**Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA)** — LTGP > CAC is necessary but the
*first* purchase's profit is usually less than CAC, and full LTGP trickles in
over months. That cash-flow gap caps scale. Fix: make a customer's 30-day
gross profit cover CAC + cost to serve (any business can get 30-day
interest-free money on a credit card; clear it, repeat). Hormozi's fix in the
book is an immediate upsell that closes the gap. This is the bridge to
$100M Money Models.

## More / Better / New + the constraint

When a channel works and you want more leads:
1. **More** — do more of what already works, up to capacity. Even with zero
   improvement, doubling inputs roughly doubles leads. Most businesses
   under-do this and jump to "new" far too early ("size of the pie fallacy":
   they mistake the tiny slice they advertise to for the whole market).
2. **Better** — improve conversion at the **constraint** (the step with the
   biggest drop-off; smallest fix there = biggest total gain). **Test one
   thing per week per platform.** Each week: pick winners, **write the result
   in a cumulative test log** (so you start "a zillion improvements later, not
   at square one"), design the next test to beat the current best. If the
   current best survives **~4 tries / one month**, move testing to the next
   constraint. Front-of-funnel steps (call-out, offer, CTA, headline) are
   usually the constraint.
3. **New** — only once more + better are tapped: **new placement** (same
   platform) → **new platform** → **new Core Four activity**. Order by
   leads-per-unit-work.

Under $1M/yr profit: do **more** first (not enough volume for % changes to
matter). Above $1M/yr: flip to **better** first (lowest cost, highest return).

## The Rule of 100 and Open to Goal

**Rule of 100**: 100 primary lead-getting actions per day, for 100 days
straight. (100 reach-outs; or 100 minutes of content; or a set ad spend + 100
minutes making ads.) It's a forcing function against the real failure mode —
doing far too little, too inconsistently, to learn anything. Most people do a
fraction of the required volume ("100 over six weeks" instead of "100 per
day") then conclude it doesn't work.

**Open to Goal**: commit to the *work* until you hit a specific *outcome*
count, no matter how long it takes. The next level up from Rule of 100.

## Lead Getters

Leverage = output per unit of your time. Four kinds of people who advertise
for you (not "things you do" — you use the Core Four to *get* them):
1. **Customers / referrals** — lowest cost, highest LTGP, exponential (one
   brings two brings four). Referral growth equation: referrals in vs. churn
   out; if referrals % > churn %, you compound with no other advertising.
   Most businesses fail here because (a) the product isn't as good as they
   think, and (b) they don't ask. Engineer it: ask at peak delight, give a
   reason, reward both sides, one step. Aim for **25%+ of customers from
   referrals** before scaling paid advertising again.
2. **Employees** — hire people whose job is lead-gen, once there's a
   repeatable process to hand over. Hire **experience, not potential** for the
   person running an operationally complex channel.
3. **Agencies** — pay specialists to run a channel, ideally while you learn it
   from them (not a permanent black box).
4. **Affiliates / partners** — others who already have your audience, for a
   cut. Needs LTGP:CAC headroom for the commission.
Referrals first; the rest cost money or management.

## The seven levels

1. Friends know (warm outreach). 2. Everyone you know knows, consistently
   (warm outreach + content, maxing personal capacity). 3. Employees help you
   do more. 4. Product is good enough for consistent referrals (≥25%) — fix
   product first, then scale team. 5. More places, more ways, more people
   (new placements → new platform → new activity). 6. Hire experienced
   executives to own channels. 7. (Hormozi hasn't written it yet.)

Realistic timeline to a $100M leads machine, for someone who knows what to
do: 5–10 years.

  "reveal the problem" types.

  free-to-try framing, price/guarantee/link held for the reply (big fast

  (permission vs. free-to-try) and the weekly subject-line test are "better"
  work. Reading level: worth confirming scripts are near 3rd-grade (the ~50%
  lift finding).

  then a DM referencing the email). On-model.
- **More / Better / New, as applied 2026-09-07** — the marketing plan is
  built on this explicitly. **More** = fixed 100/day first-touch + uncapped
  follow-ups (Rule-of-100 shaped). **Better** = one weekly A/B test on one
  asset + a cumulative Test Log; 4 unbeaten weekly tests = established winner,
  move to the next asset. **New** = explicitly deferred. Followed closely.
  casdey is under $1M profit, so "more first" is the right ordering.
- **Constraint** — casdey's funnel drop-off is at *reply → engaged*
  (2.0% engaged vs. 3% target as of the last snapshot). That's where the

  "first customers prove where they come from" — on-model (paid ads last).
- **Lead Getters** — none active. Referrals are the natural first one; the

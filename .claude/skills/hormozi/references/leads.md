## What a lead is

A lead is not a contact — it's someone who has *engaged* with your stuff. The
job isn't "get leads", it's "get engaged leads", then "get them to give you
contact info", then "get them to buy". Volume of names means nothing without
engagement.

## Lead magnets

A lead magnet is a small, complete solution to a narrow problem, given free (or
near-free) to start the relationship. Good ones:
- solve one real problem completely, and reveal the next problem (which your
  paid thing solves),
- are fast to consume and give a quick win,
- are things people would pay for,
- match the buyer you actually want.
Formats: a tool/calculator, a sample/trial, a checklist, a mini-audit, a
"reveal the problem" report. The free tier of a SaaS is a lead magnet if it
delivers a real, complete win and exposes the next need.

## The Core Four

Every way to get a lead is one of four, formed by a 2×2: **who** you contact
(people who know you = warm / strangers = cold) × **how many at once**
(one-to-one = private message / one-to-many = public post or ad).

|              | One-to-one (private) | One-to-many (public) |
| ------------ | -------------------- | -------------------- |
| **Warm**     | Warm outreach        | Post free content    |
| **Cold**     | Cold outreach        | Paid ads             |

1. **Warm outreach** — personally message people who already know you. Cheapest,
   fastest to first customer, no audience or budget required. Hormozi's default
   starting point. The ACA loop: **A**cknowledge them, **C**ompliment,
   **A**sk about their situation — a real conversation, not a pitch, then offer
   only if it fits.
2. **Post free content** — publish value publicly so strangers become warm.
   Slow to start, compounding, builds an owned audience and trust at scale.
   "Give away the secrets, sell the implementation."
3. **Cold outreach** — privately message strangers at volume (email, DM, calls).
   Works with no audience and no ad budget, just effort and a list. Needs
   personalisation, a big fast-value hook, and relentless follow-up. Volume ×
   quality of list × quality of message.
4. **Paid ads** — pay a platform to put your offer in front of strangers.
   Fastest to scale, costs money, needs a working offer and funnel first or you
   just buy losses faster.

You don't need all four. Get *one* working to a predictable lead-per-day before
adding the next. Adding a channel before the current one works just splits
attention across two things that don't work.

## Doing each of the Core Four

Common shape across all four:
- **Personalise / target** — right list, right person, a reason it's them.
- **Big fast value in the hook** — lead with something worth their attention in
  the first line (a useful observation, a specific offer, a free thing), not
  "hope you're well, let me introduce myself".
- **Call to action, singular** — one next step, easy, low-friction.
- **Follow up** — most replies and sales come from follow-ups, not first touch.
  Multi-touch, multi-channel (email then DM, referencing the email), then a
  final "walk-away" message that names itself as the last one. Then stop.
- **Measure the funnel, not the vanity metric** — contacted → replied →
  positive → call/trial → customer, and the conversion between each step.

## LTGP : CAC

- **LTGP** = lifetime *gross profit* per customer (revenue minus cost to serve,
  over the whole relationship — not revenue, not first payment).
- **CAC** = fully-loaded cost to acquire one customer (spend + tools + time /
  customers won).
- Aim for **LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1**. Below ~3:1 there isn't enough margin to fund
  growth, overhead and risk. At/above it, every customer clearly pays for the
  next.
- Two ways to win: raise LTGP (price, retention, upsells, lower cost to serve)
  or lower CAC (better list, better message, better conversion, referrals).
- The ratio is what licenses you to spend on ads. If it's healthy and you're
  not scaling, the constraint is nerve or operations, not economics.

## More / Better / New

When a channel works and you want more leads, in order:
1. **More** — do more of what's already working. More outreach volume, more
   posts, more budget, more hours. Most businesses under-do this and jump to
   "new" too early. Exhaust "more" first.
2. **Better** — improve conversion at each step: the list, the hook, the CTA,
   the offer framing, the follow-up sequence, the call. Small lifts compound
   across the funnel. Run one test at a time so you know what moved it.
3. **New** — only once "more" and "better" are tapped: add a new channel, a new
   audience, a new lead magnet, a new Lead Getter.

## Lead Getters — get others to get leads for you

Four "who else can generate leads":
1. **Customers** — referrals and word of mouth. Engineer it: ask at the moment
   of delight, give a reason (reciprocity, reward both sides), make it one
   step. A referral has near-zero CAC and higher trust.
2. **Employees** — hire people whose job is lead generation once you can afford
   to and have a repeatable process to hand them.
3. **Agencies** — pay specialists to run a channel, ideally while you learn it
   from them, not as a permanent black box.
4. **Affiliates / partners** — other people who already have your audience and
   promote you for a cut. Great when your LTGP:CAC has room for the commission.

Referrals first — cheapest and best trust. The others cost money or management
and only make sense once the core loop works.

## The Rule of 100

For the first stretch, do **100 primary lead-generating actions per day** — 100
outreach messages, or 100 minutes of content, or a set ad spend — for 100 days
straight. It's a forcing function against the real failure mode, which is doing
too little too inconsistently to learn anything. Consistency and volume beat
cleverness early.

## casdey application

Where casdey stands against this framework. Keep in sync with the "Marketing
plan (Hormozi)" and "Stage 1" sections of `CLAUDE.md` and with
`casdey-ledger.md`.

- **Channel choice:** casdey runs **cold outreach** as the primary channel
  (email + IG DM), deliberately skipping warm. Reason on file: Davide has no
  warm list among gym owners; cold fits casdey's and his situation. This is a
  recorded, accepted divergence from Hormozi's "warm first" — see the ledger.
- **Second channel:** IG content/presence exists but is thin. Per "one channel
  first", cold email is the one being pushed to work; content is not yet a
  real second channel and shouldn't be treated as one until cold is
  predictable.
- **More / Better / New, as applied 2026-09-07:** the marketing plan is
  explicitly built on this. **More** = first-touch raised to a fixed 100/day
  with uncapped follow-ups (Rule-of-100 shaped). **Better** = one structured
  A/B test per week on one asset (subject, hook, CTA, framing), with a
  cumulative test log so each week builds on the current best; 4 unbeaten
  weekly tests = established winner, move on. **New** = explicitly deferred
  until More/Better are tapped. This is the framework followed closely.
- **Hook / value:** cold email leads with the lapsed-member angle and the
  free-to-try framing; price, guarantee and link are held for the reply, not
  the first touch. Matches "big fast value, single CTA, don't overload touch
  one".
- **Follow-up:** two follow-ups per lead plus a final walk-away, multi-channel
  (email then a DM that references the email). Matches the framework.
- **Lead magnet:** the **Free tier** is casdey's lead magnet — import your
  list, see who lapsed and what they're worth, can't send. Complete small win
  (the number), reveals the next problem (now actually win them back), which
  the paid tiers solve. This is a well-formed lead magnet in Hormozi's terms.
- **LTGP:CAC:** not yet computed from real data — no paying gym, CAC is
  currently founder time only. When the first paid gyms land, compute
  LTGP:CAC before any thought of paid ads. The plan already gates ads on
  "first customers prove where they come from".
- **Lead Getters:** none active. Referrals are the natural first one and the
  go-to-market notes already flag pushing a referral offer at the point of
  converting a gym to paid. Nothing built yet.
- **Rule of 100:** first-touch at 100/day meets it on volume. The 100-day
  consistency part is on the routines, which run daily.
